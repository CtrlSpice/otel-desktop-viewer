# Home welcome facts

`GettingStarted.svelte` chooses one welcome line when it mounts. Four lines are
playful taglines; four are paraphrased findings from peer-reviewed axolotl research.

## Citation convention

Each scientific fact has a short author, journal, year and DOI citation beside it
in the source. This reference list records the displayed wording, the full paper
reference and any context needed to interpret the claim. When changing a fact,
update both its source comment and this list. Keep the wording within the species,
tissue and developmental stage studied. DOI links identify the original papers.

## Facts and references

### Genome size

> An axolotl’s genome is about ten times the size of a human’s.

Nowoshilow, S., et al. (2018). **The axolotl genome and the evolution of key tissue
formation regulators.** *Nature*, 554, 50–55.
<https://doi.org/10.1038/nature25458>

The paper reports a 32-gigabase-pair genome and explicitly compares it with the
human genome. The comparison concerns DNA length, not the number of genes.

### Immune cells and limb regeneration

> Axolotls need immune cells called macrophages to regrow a limb.

Godwin, J. W., Pinto, A. R., & Rosenthal, N. A. (2013). **Macrophages are required
for adult salamander limb regeneration.** *Proceedings of the National Academy of
Sciences*, 110(23), 9415–9420.
<https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1300290110>

The experiments used axolotls. Depleting macrophages early after limb amputation
blocked regeneration despite wound closure; the requirement is timing-dependent.

### Forebrain regeneration

> Axolotls can replace lost neurons and rebuild connections in parts of their forebrain.

Lust, K., et al. (2022). **Single-cell analyses of axolotl telencephalon
organization, neurogenesis, and regeneration.** *Science*, 377(6610), eabp9262.
<https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abp9262>

The study found restoration of neuron populations and axonal connections after
injury in the telencephalon. This does not establish regeneration of every brain
region or complete recovery of every brain function.

### A developmental limit on lens regeneration

> Axolotls lose the ability to regrow an eye lens about two weeks after hatching.

Suetsugu-Maki, R., et al. (2012). **Lens regeneration in axolotl: new evidence of
developmental plasticity.** *BMC Biology*, 10, 103.
<https://doi.org/10.1186/1741-7007-10-103>

Lens regeneration was observed during a limited early developmental window,
roughly the first two weeks after hatching under the study conditions. This is an
approximate developmental window, not a universal cutoff at exactly fourteen days.
